Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about our evidence-grounded AI verification architecture.

TruthLens uses a multi-layered pipeline: it extracts grammatical claims using syntactic analysis, scores sensationalism and clickbait patterns using lexical features and fine-tuned BERT embeddings, verifies originating publisher transparency, and cross-references assertions against public repositories such as Reuters, the Associated Press, WHO, and peer-reviewed databases.

We explicitly separate the machine learning probability score (which evaluates writing style and linguistic manipulation) from the factual grounding status (which reflects whether physical or official evidence directly supports or contradicts the claim). An article can have sensational writing but still be factually true, or be calmly written while asserting completely fabricated facts.

Our models are calibrated on balanced cross-spectrum benchmark datasets (including LIAR, FEVER, and FakeNewsCorpus). Claims are evaluated against primary empirical records (government gazettes, satellite telemetry, scientific registries) rather than editorial op-eds.
